Base Impact joined a group from Rock Church to help a member finish rebuilding their home after the loss of a family member. This is a story about community showing up in a time of grief — not just with words, but with hands and tools.

## The situation

A member of Rock Church lost a family member and was in the middle of a home project that had stalled. Grief, combined with the practical challenge of completing home repairs alone, left the member overwhelmed. The church rallied a group to help, and Base Impact joined to contribute labor and support.

## What we did

- **Home repairs** — Helped complete unfinished work including installing an air vent/return cover and other finishing tasks
- **Labor and tools** — Provided hands and equipment to move the project forward
- **Presence and support** — Being there as a community, offering encouragement during a difficult time
- **Follow-up** — Checking in after the work day to make sure the member had what they needed

## The impact

- **A home closer to finished** — Practical progress on repairs that had stalled
- **Community connection** — The member saw that people cared enough to show up and help
- **Grief support** — Sometimes the most important thing isn't the repair — it's knowing you're not alone
- **A model for others** — Showing how churches and nonprofits can partner to serve neighbors in need

## Why this matters

Grief and home repair don't usually appear in the same sentence, but they often go together. When someone loses a family member, the practical tasks of life don't pause. A community that shows up with tools and presence — not just sympathy — can make a real difference in someone's ability to move forward.
