Base Impact joined a cold weather shelter training session to learn how to set up and run overnight shelter for neighbors when temperatures drop dangerously low. The training covered everything from cot setup to guest welcome procedures.

## The training

Volunteers gathered at a community center for hands-on training on operating a cold weather shelter. The session covered:

- **Shelter setup** — Arranging cots, blankets, and supplies in a large hall
- **Guest intake** — Welcoming neighbors, checking in, and assigning sleeping areas
- **Safety protocols** — Fire safety, health screening, and emergency procedures
- **Hospitality** — Creating a warm, respectful environment for guests
- **Team coordination** — Roles, shifts, and communication during overnight operations

## What we learned

The training reinforced that shelter work isn't just about a warm place to sleep — it's about dignity. Every guest is a neighbor, and the experience should reflect that. From the first greeting to the morning departure, the goal is to make people feel safe, respected, and cared for.

## Base Impact's role

Base Impact supports cold weather shelter efforts by:
- **Providing volunteers** trained and ready to serve
- **Connecting resources** — Using our directory to help shelter guests find longer-term services
- **Technology support** — Helping shelter organizations with device and internet needs
- **Community awareness** — Spreading the word about when shelters are open and how to access them

## When shelters open

Cold weather shelters in Brevard County typically open when nighttime temperatures are forecast to drop below a threshold (often around 40°F). Announcements are made through local media, 211, and community organizations. If you or someone you know needs shelter on a cold night, call 211 or check with local churches and community centers.
