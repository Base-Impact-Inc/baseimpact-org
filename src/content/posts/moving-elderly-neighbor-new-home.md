On October 27, 2026, Base Impact helped a 73-year-old neighbor move out of her car and into a room of her own. It took a housing search, a conversation between two strangers, and a day of hard work — and it ended with one neighbor finally having a comfortable place to call home.

## The situation

A 73-year-old neighbor in Brevard County was living in her car. She needed a safe, stable place to stay, but finding housing at her age — and on her terms — wasn't simple. We started looking around with her, checking what was available and who might be willing to take her in.

## Finding a match

The right fit came from an 83-year-old woman living alone who had a room to spare. She agreed to talk with our neighbor, and the two of them connected. That conversation led to an offer: our neighbor could move in.

That kind of match doesn't happen through a listing. It happens when someone is willing to open their door and take a chance on another person — and when we help make the introduction possible.

## What we did

Once the arrangement was agreed, our volunteers helped with the practical work of moving in:

- **Moving boxes and furniture** — carrying her belongings into the new room so she didn't have to do the heavy lifting alone
- **Cleaning the room** — getting the space ready so it was clean, comfortable, and truly livable before she moved in
- **Setting up** — arranging her things so she could settle in without struggling

The goal was simple: make the transition easier and let her start from a place of comfort, not exhaustion.

## Why this matters

Housing insecurity doesn't always look like a shelter. Sometimes it's a 73-year-old sleeping in her car, one conversation away from a room. Our role was small but concrete — help find the opening, connect two neighbors, and do the physical work that made the move possible.

It also reflects what Base Impact is about. Technology access and community care go hand in hand: the neighbors we serve with devices and digital skills are often facing housing instability and isolation too. When we can help, we show up.
