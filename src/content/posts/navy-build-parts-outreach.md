This is what a request for help can look like: not a person standing at a pantry door, but a workbench, a screwdriver, and an open PC case on a cutting mat.

The photo came with an email from an active-duty Navy member serving near Jacksonville. He is working toward aerospace engineering and has built most of his first desktop himself — a machine intended for CAD and 3D modeling. Someone had already donated an 8 GB DDR5 memory stick. What he still needed was a working used graphics card, more compatible RAM, and three case fans.

He knew our technology access program is based in Brevard County. He asked, politely, whether we knew of a hardware reuse group or community partner who could help an active-duty learner in Northeast Florida.

## What we could actually do

We did not have a graphics card to hand him, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. What we could do was make the search shorter, and more honest, than the web search he had already tried.

So we did three things:

- **Mapped the gap.** Most national "free computer" listings are about internet discounts or whole devices. His need was component-level: a GPU, DDR5 RAM, fans. Those are much harder to find published anywhere.
- **Updated our directory.** We added nine listings across technology and money help, including a Jacksonville reuse store, veteran-focused refurbishers, a national IT reuse outlet, nonprofit device sellers, and military financial-support programs. Each one now carries a source link, eligibility notes, and the date we checked it.
- **Kept the door open.** We asked him to keep us updated — what part turns up, what fits, what does not. If the first lead fails, we want the next one to be better informed.

That follow-up matters. A directory is only useful if someone comes back and tells us what actually worked.

## Where we pointed him first

**In Northeast Florida**

[ReUse IT Jax](https://www.reuseitjax.com/) is the closest match to the actual parts list. It is a Jacksonville reuse store and the retail arm of a recycling operation: used PCs, phones, cords, cameras and parts at low prices; a workbench where you can test items before buying; free hard-drive shredding with a certificate of destruction; and paid diagnostics, installs and upgrades. They also collect sign-ups for PC giveaways and have an item-request form. Inventory moves, so call before driving out.

The [Fleet and Family Support Center at NAS Jacksonville](https://cnrse.cnic.navy.mil/Installations/NAS-Jacksonville/Fleet-and-Family-Readiness/Support-Services/) offers personal financial counseling to service members from E-1 up, and [Navy-Marine Corps Relief Society](https://www.nmcrs.org/get-help/financial-assistance/interest-free-loans-grants) provides interest-free loans and grants to active-duty Sailors and Marines. Either may be an option if a small no-interest advance would cover the remaining parts.

**For veteran-specific technology**

[Tech For Troops](https://www.techfortroops.org/) publishes eligibility around veterans, Guard members and transitioning active-duty personnel. Recent coverage also describes laptops going to active-duty military in need, so our advice was to call and explain the situation rather than self-disqualify. [Cell Phones For Soldiers](https://cellphonesforsoldiers.com/) serves active-duty members with communication support and emergency funding, and [Human-I-T](https://www.human-i-t.org/low-cost-devices/) offers extra discounts for veterans in need on refurbished devices.

**For the wider pipeline**

[Computers Advancing Education](http://www.computersadvancingeducation.org/) in Titusville is an all-volunteer group that refurbishes donated machines and gives them free to people in need — and it takes parts and partners. [World Computer Exchange](https://worldcomputerexchange.org/get-involved/give-computers/) and [PCs for People](https://www.pcsforpeople.org/nonprofits/) handle device donation and low-cost refurbished computers at scale, and [Compudopt](https://www.compudopt.org/) runs free-computer programs in participating communities.

None of these is a promise of a GPU. Every one is a real door to knock on, with a name and a number attached.

## How to help with this build

If you have a working used graphics card, compatible DDR5 RAM, or three case fans sitting in a drawer, we would like to hear from you. So would the Sailor who is trying to finish this machine.

- **Contact us** through the [contact form](/contact) and mention "CAD build." Tell us the part, the specs, and whether you can ship or arrange a handoff. We will pass useful leads along and keep a note of what fits.
- **Donate** through our [donate page](/donate). If you would rather fund parts than ship them, that works too — flexible funds are what let us say yes when a specific component turns up.
- **Volunteer** if you can verify compatibility, check a motherboard's manual, or help a service member troubleshoot a build over a video call. That kind of expertise is worth more than a box of cables.
- **Spread the word** to local repair shops, makerspaces, and school tech programs in Northeast Florida. The right used GPU may be two towns away from someone who knows he needs it.

We will share an update when he writes back. That was part of the request: keep in touch, and let us know what worked.
