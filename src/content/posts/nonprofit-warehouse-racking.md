Base Impact volunteers rolled up their sleeves to help build storage racking in a nonprofit warehouse. The project created organized, accessible storage space for donated goods, supplies, and community resources — the kind of behind-the-scenes infrastructure that keeps nonprofits running.

## The project

A local nonprofit needed better storage organization. Donated items, supplies, and equipment were piling up without a proper system, making it hard to find what was needed when it was needed. The solution: install heavy-duty steel racking to create vertical storage space.

## What we did

- **Assembled racking** — Bolted together steel beams and crossbars to create sturdy shelving units
- **Organized storage** — Sorted and placed donated goods, supplies, and equipment on the new racks
- **Safety checks** — Ensured all racking was level, secure, and within weight limits
- **Labeled and inventoried** — Marked shelves and created a basic inventory system for easy retrieval

## The impact

With proper storage racking in place, the nonprofit can now:
- **Store more** — Vertical storage dramatically increases capacity
- **Find things faster** — Organized shelves mean less time searching
- **Protect donations** — Items stay clean, dry, and accessible
- **Serve more people** — Better organization means faster distribution of resources
- **Scale operations** — Room to grow as donations and needs increase

## Why infrastructure matters

Nonprofits often focus on direct service — and rightly so. But without the right infrastructure (storage, organization, logistics), even the best intentions can bottleneck. A few hours of volunteer labor building racking can multiply a nonprofit's capacity for months or years.
