A few months back, Base Impact had the opportunity to help travelers and neighbors in ways that went beyond our technology mission. Sometimes the most important thing you can do is show up with food, water, and a ride.

## Travelers on the Mims bike path

While near a bike path in Mims, FL, we met travelers who were making their way south toward South Carolina. They were on foot and by bike, carrying what they could, and the Florida heat was unforgiving. We stopped to talk with them, learned their story, and did what we could to help.

### What we provided
- **Food** — Sandwiches, snacks, and ready-to-eat items to keep them going
- **Water** — Plenty of water bottles to stay hydrated in the heat
- **Conversation** — Sometimes the most valuable thing is just listening and treating people with dignity
- **Information** — Directions, resource locations, and tips for the road ahead

These travelers were on a long journey, and a small act of kindness — a meal, cold water, and someone who cared — made a real difference in their day.

## Giving rides in the Florida heat

Over the past few months, we've also given rides to neighbors who were walking in the brutal Florida heat. When you see someone walking along a highway in 90+ degree weather with no shade and no water, you stop.

### People we've helped
- **Neighbors walking to work** — People without reliable transportation who walk miles each day
- **Seniors walking to appointments** — Elderly residents trying to reach medical or social services
- **Families walking to stores** — Parents with children trying to get groceries or supplies
- **People without homes** — Individuals walking to find shelter, food, or a safe place to rest

### What a ride means
For someone without a car in rural Brevard County, a ride isn't just convenience — it's safety. Walking in Florida heat can lead to heat exhaustion, dehydration, and dangerous situations. A 10-minute ride can prevent a medical emergency.

## Why this matters to Base Impact

Our core mission is technology access — devices, internet, and digital skills. But we're a community organization first. When we see a neighbor in need, whether it's a laptop that won't boot or a person walking in dangerous heat, we help.

Technology and community care go hand in hand. The same neighbors who need tech help often also need:
- **Transportation** to get to work, appointments, or services
- **Food and water** to get through the day
- **Someone to talk to** who treats them with respect
- **A connection** to resources and services
