Base Impact helped a war veteran by completely reloading and setting up his laptop. This is one of the stories that shows why our technology access work matters — sometimes a working device is the difference between isolation and connection.

## The situation

A war veteran in Brevard County had a laptop that had become unusable. It was slow, cluttered with outdated software, and running an old operating system that was no longer supported. He couldn't access his email, browse the web, or use online services. For someone who relies on digital tools to stay connected with family, access VA benefits, and manage daily life, this was a serious problem.

## What we did

Our volunteers took the laptop and:
- **Backed up important files** — Photos, documents, and personal data were safely preserved
- **Reinstalled the operating system** — A clean, up-to-date OS was installed
- **Installed essential software** — Web browser, email client, PDF reader, and security tools
- **Set up security** — Antivirus, firewall, and automatic updates configured
- **Configured accessibility** — Larger text and simplified interface for easier use
- **Trained the veteran** — Walked him through using the refreshed laptop, accessing email, and browsing the web

## The impact

With a working laptop, this veteran can now:
- **Stay connected** with family and friends through email and video calls
- **Access VA benefits** and healthcare portals online
- **Manage finances** and pay bills digitally
- **Find community resources** through our directory
- **Stay informed** with news and information

## Why veterans need tech help

Many veterans — especially older ones — face a digital divide. They may have served before the internet era and are now expected to navigate complex online systems for benefits, healthcare, and communication. A working device with proper setup and training can be life-changing.
