A neighbor sits across the table with a housing application on their phone. There is a landlord letter to read, a benefits question to answer, and a deadline on Friday. The volunteer helping them has a laptop, a notepad, and about forty minutes.

That forty minutes is where we keep seeing the same gap. There is useful technology that could read the letter, explain the question in plain language, and help draft an answer. Most of it lives in someone else's cloud, and using it means uploading a person's documents to a service we do not control. For someone facing housing insecurity, that is not a fair trade.

So we are asking for a different kind of tool: computers powerful enough to run useful AI models locally, in the room, with a volunteer and a neighbor in charge. The full specification lives on our [AI hardware campaign page](/ai-hardware-campaign). This article explains why we are asking and what we would do with the machines.

## What "unified memory" has to do with it

Local AI models hold their working knowledge in memory while they run. The more memory a machine has, the larger the model it can run and the more it can handle at once. On many modern laptops and workstations, the CPU and GPU share one pool, called unified memory. That shared pool is why a thin laptop can do work that once needed a rack of separate parts.

Our working floor is 32 GB. That is enough to be genuinely useful for reading documents and drafting text. Our target is 64 GB to 128 GB, with 128 GB as the ideal. At that level a single machine can run a more capable model, keep several documents in view, and serve more than one volunteer.

These are not hypothetical specifications. Apple's published [MacBook Pro specifications](https://www.apple.com/macbook-pro/liquid-retina-xdr-display/specs/) currently list M5 Max configurations with up to 128 GB of unified memory. [AMD's Ryzen AI Max+ 395](https://www.amd.com/en/products/processors/laptop/ryzen/ai-300-series/amd-ryzen-ai-max-plus-395.html) supports up to 128 GB of system memory. We checked both on 2026-11-27. We are not asking for a particular brand. We are describing a class of machine that exists today, from more than one manufacturer, and we will happily evaluate refurbished or open-box units that meet the memory floor.

## Three ways a gift can fit

- **A portable laptop.** A 32 GB minimum, ideally 64–128 GB, with a solid-state drive and a battery that lasts a home visit. This is the machine that goes to a shelter, a pantry, or a kitchen table.
- **A desk-side workstation.** 64–128 GB of memory, faster storage, and room to run document processing, website builds, and custom scripts. It also backs up the laptops.
- **A server at the office.** 128 GB or more of system memory, or GPUs with substantial video memory, reachable over our own network. One shared box can serve several volunteers without a per-seat cloud bill.

Docks, external drives, surge protectors, and UPS units matter too. A donated laptop without power protection or a way to carry it is only half a solution. If you have a system and are not sure where it lands, [send us the specs](/contact) and we will give you a straight answer.

## What we would actually do with it

The first job is form filling. Our volunteers already walk neighbors through housing portals, benefit renewals, and job applications. A local assistant can read a question aloud, explain what an agency means by "household income," and help draft an answer. It never submits anything. The volunteer checks the work, and the neighbor decides what is true.

The second job is research. "Who can help with rent this week?" deserves an answer built from verified local sources with links that can be opened later, not a generic response. Our [resource directory](/directory) and [guide](/guide) are the starting point; local tooling will help us search and explain them faster.

The third job is capacity for small nonprofits. A church, pantry, or 1–5 person outreach group often needs a website, a repaired page, a donation form, or a small script that saves staff time. We already support these groups through our [nonprofit technology program](/programs/nonprofit-tech). Better local hardware lets us do more of that work in-house, at no cost to them. The same machine helps us write clearer grant narratives and reports, building on the [AI Grant Toolkit](/grants/ai-toolkit) we are developing.

## Why rural development belongs in the same conversation

Help with a form is immediate, but it is not the whole picture. In a rural town, a person who starts a small business, finds better work, or learns a marketable skill changes more than their own circumstances. They bring income into the area, hire someone, and become the person who can help the next neighbor. That is why we are treating entrepreneurship and employment as part of this campaign, not as a separate wish list.

The practical version is unglamorous. A founder needs a business plan, a name, a price, a product description, and a page on the internet that works on a phone. A job seeker needs a résumé that matches the posting, a practice interview, and help understanding an application portal. An existing small business needs quotes, flyers, a spreadsheet that makes sense, and someone to fix the website nobody has touched in four years. A local assistant can draft, edit, and explain all of that while the human being keeps the decisions.

We want those tools to stay local in two senses: the documents should not be uploaded to a stranger's service, and the skills should stay in the community. The goal is not to import a tech scene. It is for local people to own the work, keep the revenue, and be able to teach the next person. The full strategy, including what we would report to funders, is on the [campaign page](/ai-hardware-campaign).

## The Life Group and the teaching part

We are starting a Life Group for people who want to join us in these ventures: job seekers, founders, programmers, tradespeople, students, and anyone willing to learn and contribute. It is a regular peer group with a practical agenda. Members work on real projects, share skills, pair an idea with someone who can help build it, and follow up on what they said they would do. It is not a job guarantee, a sales funnel, or a class you sit through passively. There is no fee, and no prior technology experience is required. You can [join us](/join) or [ask about the group](/contact).

The donated machines double as teaching machines. The workshops we want to run cover AI literacy and safe use, prompting for real tasks, beginner programming and automation, websites and online presence, a plain-language introduction to machine learning, and the workplace skills that keep a job or business running: email, documents, spreadsheets, video calls, and online portals. We will publish dates when venues and instructors are confirmed. Libraries, schools, chambers of commerce, faith communities, and employers can [offer a room or a skill](/contact); we will supply the curriculum and the hardware.

None of this has produced outcomes yet. It is a stated plan with a stated measurement approach, which is what funders need to see before they trust a small nonprofit with equipment.

## Why we are being this specific about care

A computer that handles someone's ID, income, and address has to be handled carefully. Every donated device will be logged by serial number, wiped and reinstalled before use, asset-tagged, kept updated, and access-controlled. We will report how it is used to the donor. We do not publish recipient names or documents, and we only use photos and stories with consent. At the end of a machine's life, we reuse it inside the program first and recycle it responsibly.

The full five-step stewardship plan is on the [campaign page](/ai-hardware-campaign). We wrote it before asking for anything so that funders could see the controls, not just the wish list.
