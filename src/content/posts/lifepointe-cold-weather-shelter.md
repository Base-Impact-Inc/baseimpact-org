When the temperature drops in Brevard County, a safe place to sleep is not a luxury — it is basic safety. On September 19, Base Impact joined volunteers at [LifePointe Ministries](https://www.lifepointeministries.org/) in Titusville for a hands-on training day focused on one question: how do we open an inclement weather shelter quickly, respectfully, and well?

The answer was not complicated, but it was practical. Cots have to be set up. Guests need a calm welcome. Volunteers need to know their roles before the doors open.

## Why this shelter matters

LifePointe Ministries, at 4220 South Hopkins Avenue, operates the only inclement-weather shelter between Titusville and Port Orange.[16] The ministry and the [Titusville Civic Center](https://titusvilleciviccenter.com/lifepointe-ministries) provide warm food, a change of clothing, blankets, showers, and hygiene items — needs that go well beyond a place to sleep.[16]

The work is volunteer-run from top to bottom. Pastor Royce "Scooter" Morrison has said the outreach is "100% volunteer-based" and that even the pastor draws no pay for it.[19] During one recent cold spell, the shelter was open 12 of 26 days, and all 60-plus beds were full.[19]

That kind of demand is why training days matter. A shelter is only as ready as the people who know what to do when the call comes.

The training-day flyer spelled out the purpose plainly: learn how the shelter operates, ask questions, and find a role before the weather turns.

![Cots set up for the inclement weather shelter training day](/assets/lifepointe-cold-weather-cots.jpg)

## CREW 29: the team behind the work

LifePointe's outreach arm is called C.R.E.W. 29 — a weekly gathering of volunteers who run showers, hand out clothing from the church warehouse, provide a warm meal, and help neighbors with practical and spiritual needs.[17] The name appears on the green lanyards volunteers wear. LifePointe describes the team as "a large group effort" drawing support from area churches.[22]

Training with CREW 29 meant learning the shelter from the inside:

- **Cot setup** — moving cots into place and arranging a hall for overnight guests
- **Intake and welcome** — receiving guests respectfully and assigning sleeping space
- **Hospitality** — meals, showers, clean clothing, and a calm, dignified environment
- **Team coordination** — how shifts, volunteers, and donations come together on short notice

## Where Base Impact fits

Base Impact is not the organization running this shelter. LifePointe is. Our role is to help make the work around it easier and more connected.

Shelter work and technology access overlap more than people expect. A guest trying to rebuild a life needs to reach benefits, family, housing resources, and health care. The organization opening its doors needs working devices, reliable information, and a way to tell the community when shelter is available.

That is the value we bring:

- **Trained volunteers** who understand the basics before a cold night arrives
- **Resource navigation** through our [directory](/directory), helping guests find longer-term services after the immediate need is met
- **Technology support** for the nonprofits and churches doing the work
- **Community awareness** that shares accurate, timely shelter information with neighbors who need it

We learn from partners like LifePointe, then use our platform to make their work more visible and more useful to the people they serve.

## How neighbors can help

Inclement weather shelters in Brevard County typically open when severe weather is forecast — most often when overnight temperatures are expected to fall to around 40°F — with announcements through local media, 211, and community organizations. If you or someone you know needs a place on a cold night, call **211**.

LifePointe is always looking for volunteers and supplies. Call **321-383-0254** or visit [lifepointeministries.org](https://www.lifepointeministries.org/).[16][17]
